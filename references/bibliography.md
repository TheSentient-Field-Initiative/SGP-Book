# Annotated Bibliography

What each source supports — and what it does not. External sources establish background science only; none endorses SGP. Institute sources are cited with file:line precision; "unpublished working file" marks records that are not peer-reviewed publications.

## Quantum entanglement and Bell correlations (Chapter 15)

- Einstein, Podolsky, Rosen (1935). "Can quantum-mechanical description of physical reality be considered complete?" *Physical Review* 47:777–780. Supports: the original incompleteness argument. Does not support: any verdict on completeness (that comes from Bell + experiment).
- Bell, J. S. (1964). "On the Einstein Podolsky Rosen paradox." *Physics Physique Fizika* 1(3):195–200. DOI: 10.1103/PhysicsPhysiqueFizika.1.195 (verified via APS record). Supports: local hidden-variable models satisfying Bell's assumptions cannot reproduce QM statistics. Does not support: faster-than-light signaling; any specific interpretation of QM.
- Clauser, Horne, Shimony, Holt (1969). "Proposed experiment to test local hidden-variable theories." *Physical Review Letters* 23:880–884. Supports: the CHSH inequality form used in experiments. Background only.
- Aspect, Dalibard, Roger (1982). "Experimental test of Bell's inequalities using time-varying analyzers." *Physical Review Letters* 49:1804–1807. Supports: experimental violation with (then-)open loopholes. Background only.
- Hensen et al. (2015). "Loophole-free Bell inequality violation using electron spins separated by 1.3 kilometres." *Nature* 526:682–686. DOI: 10.1038/nature15759 (verified via Nature record; S = 2.42 ± 0.20, 245 trials). Supports: space-like separated violation closing locality + detection loopholes. Does not support: signaling; any SGP claim.
- Nielsen, M. A. & Chuang, I. L. *Quantum Computation and Quantum Information* (Cambridge). Supports: standard formalism (partial trace, purification, no-signaling as theorem of the formalism). Does not support: interpretations.
- Interpretive point (Ch. 15): "nonlocality" differs across Copenhagen / Everett / Bohm / collapse readings. Standard philosophy-of-physics observation; the book takes no interpretive side.

## Bose–Einstein condensation (Chapter 16)

- Bose, S. N. (1924). "Plancks Gesetz und Lichtquantenhypothese." *Zeitschrift für Physik* 26:178–181. Supports: counting derivation of the Planck law. Background only.
- Einstein, A. (1924/1925). "Quantentheorie des einatomigen idealen Gases." *Sitzungsberichte der Preussischen Akademie der Wissenschaften*. Supports: prediction of condensation for ideal bosons. Background only.
- Anderson, Ensher, Matthews, Wieman, Cornell (1995). "Observation of Bose-Einstein condensation in a dilute atomic vapor." *Science* 269(5221):198–201. DOI: 10.1126/science.269.5221.198 (verified via Science/PubMed records; Rb-87, ~170 nK, 2.5×10¹²/cm³, three signatures). Supports: first dilute-gas BEC observation. Does not support: any SGP claim.
- Davis et al. (1995). "Bose-Einstein condensation in a gas of sodium atoms." *Physical Review Letters* 75:3969–3973. DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevLett.75.3969. Supports: independent confirmation (sodium). Background only.
- Pethick, C. J. & Smith, H. *Bose–Einstein Condensation in Dilute Gases* (Cambridge, 2nd ed. 2008); Pitaevskii, L. & Stringari, S. *Bose-Einstein Condensation* (Oxford, 2003/2016). Support: macroscopic wavefunction / order parameter / Gross–Pitaevskii description; condensate ≠ loss of all distinctions. Background only.

## Identical particles (Chapter 17)

- Pauli, W. (1940). "The connection between spin and statistics." *Physical Review* 58:716–722. Supports: spin-statistics connection (background constraint the records cite as ordinary physics).
- Dirac, P. A. M. *The Principles of Quantum Mechanics* (Oxford); Messiah, A. *Quantum Mechanics* (Dover reprint); Sakurai, J. J. & Napolitano, J. *Modern Quantum Mechanics* (Cambridge); Griffiths, D. J. *Introduction to Quantum Mechanics*, Ch. 5 (identical particles; symmetrization). Support: symmetrized states, occupation numbers, Fock space; species identity vs numerical identity. None supports electron=proton identity.
- Particle Data Group. *Review of Particle Physics* (e.g. *PTEP* 2024). Supports: electron vs proton intrinsic properties (mass, charge, spin class, compositeness). Cited for the species table in Ch. 17.

## Spacetime emergence / holography (Chapter 18, controls only)

- Van Raamsdonk, M. (2010). "Building up spacetime with quantum entanglement." *General Relativity and Gravitation* 42:2323–2329. Cited in the records as a literature control (entanglement→geometry), explicitly NOT imported as SGP evidence. Book follows the records.
- Ryu, Takayanagi (2006). Holographic entanglement entropy. Same control-only status.

## Philosophy (Chapters 2–4, 20)

- Whitehead, A. N. *Process and Reality* (1929/1978). Cited via RD-002's comparative mapping (phenomenal-reading analogue: prehension/concrescence). The book reports RD-002's mapping; it does not newly interpret Whitehead.
- James, W. *Essays in Radical Empiricism* (1912). Same status (overlap/neutral monism analogue).
- Ladyman, J. & Ross, D. *Every Thing Must Go* (Oxford, 2007). Same status (structural-realist analogue under the functional reading).
- These readings are reported as RD-002's findings, not as the book's independent scholarship.
