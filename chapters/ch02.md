# Chapter 2. What Does Sentience Mean?

**The puzzle:** SGP starts from "sentience." But what *is* sentience here — feeling, or merely responding? The records say both, in different places. This chapter explains each meaning, shows they are not equivalent, and traces what the difference costs.

## The phenomenal formulation: the capacity to feel

The governance record — the Institute's constitutional document — puts it this way [SGP-commitment]:

> "Sentience (the capacity to feel) is the starting point of the research program, not a hypothesis to be proved." (GOVERNANCE.md:19)

The glossary repeats it verbatim ("The capacity to feel," docs/glossary/glossary.md:167). An axiomatic treatment calls sentience "the primitive character of causal interaction" and labels the claim Tier III: "not falsifiable… a philosophical axiom, not an empirical claim" (docs/axiomatic_sentience.md:7–9, 15). A companion limits-document is blunter: the key bridging claims are "NOT proven, and not mathematically provable" (docs/proof_scope_and_limits.md:77–78).

Read plainly, this is a *phenomenal* starting point: feeling, subjectivity, what-it-is-like. It is offered as a place to stand, not a hypothesis to test. You cannot run an experiment on a stipulation — and the records are admirably clear that no one should try.

## The functional formulation: the ability to respond

A later strategic document redefines the term [SGP-commitment]:

> "Sentience means the ability to respond (a functional definition of sentience, not a phenomenal one)." (RESEARCH_DIRECTOR_STRATEGIC_REORIENTATION.md:345)

Elsewhere: "SENTIENCE = ability to respond to another or itself" (SGP_RESPONSIVENESS_VS_CAPACITY_AUDIT_001.md:56); "SENTIENCE = ability to respond; response = ordered pair membership" (FOUNDATIONAL_CLARIFICATION_STAGE1.md:260).

This is a *functional* starting point: no feeling required, just response — something affecting something else, including itself. A thermostat responds. A domino responds. Under this definition, sentience is everywhere that interaction is.

## Why the difference matters

These are not two phrasings of one idea. They are different claims with different consequences — and the Institute's own coherence audit says so explicitly: "These are not equivalent… the phenomenal reading [was] never operationalized or tested… Both analyzed" (SGP_FOUNDATIONAL_PACKAGE_COHERENCE_AUDIT.md:59–61).

Consequence 1: **only the functional reading was ever tested.** Every experiment in Parts II–IV operationalizes sentience as response-as-relation. The phenomenal reading appears in governance, glossary, and axioms — and nowhere in any computation. If you believe the "capacity to feel" version, the entire experimental record is about something else.

Consequence 2: **the downstream logic flips.** Under the phenomenal reading, "reality is sentience" resembles process philosophy (Whitehead's feeling occasions), and the package is a *metaphysics of experience*. Under the functional reading, "reality is sentience" reduces to "reality is relational structure" — which is an existing position (ontic structural realism) wearing a new word. The audit's verdict: the package "oscillates between them" (RD-002:211–219).

Consequence 3: **a later document regresses.** After one audit killed "capacity" as a representational artifact (responsiveness survived; capacity did not — Exp22:360), the final portfolio decision still phrases the commitment as "the capacity to respond" (SGP_RD004_FINAL_PORTFOLIO_DECISION.md:159). Terminology, once killed, crept back in. The book notes this as a caution about how hard it is to keep words meaning one thing.

## What "responding to oneself" means

The functional definition includes self-response: something responding to itself. Mathematically, this becomes a self-loop — a pair (x, x) in a relation (Chapter 8). Philosophically, it gestures at reflexivity, self-awareness, the mind turning back on itself. The book keeps these apart: the mathematics of self-loops is trivial; the philosophy of self-consciousness is not; nothing in the records derives the second from the first.

## What sentience does *not* mean here

It does not automatically mean human consciousness, emotion, selfhood, or subjective awareness in the rich sense — at least not under the functional definition, which explicitly disavows phenomenal consciousness (RD-001:345). Under the phenomenal definition, it means feeling in some primitive sense, but the records never specify *whose* feeling or *what kind*. Both readings agree on one negative: sentience here is not a synonym for "human mind."

## Where this chapter leaves you

SGP's foundation has a documented double meaning: feeling (stipulated, never tested) and responding (tested, and — spoiler — mathematically empty, Chapter 7). The next question is what happens when you build "reality" on top of this foundation. Chapter 3 turns the picture upside down.
