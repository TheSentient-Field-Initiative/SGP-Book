# Chapter 4. A World of Experience-Content

**The proposal:** If reality is sentience, what are tables, stars, and electrons? SGP's answer: they are *experience-content* — what sentience contains, not what causes it.

## The constitutive claim

The third commitment [SGP-commitment]:

> "The physical universe is experience-content (a constitutive claim: physical objects are what sentience contains, not what sentience is caused by)." (RESEARCH_DIRECTOR_STRATEGIC_REORIENTATION.md:347)

Earlier record-language: "the 'world' as experienced," "experienced manifestation of stable mathematical organization" (see research-map 03-terminology-map). Note the dependency the reviews later exposed: this claim depends on the first two commitments *and* on actualization (C3 depends on C5 — RD-002) — because "content" that never stabilizes into anything persistent is not a world at all.

## An illustration (not evidence)

*[Author-inference — illustration.]* Consider a dream. While dreaming, mountains, people, and weather appear — vivid, structured, consequential within the dream. Nothing in the dream is *caused by* physical mountains; the mountains are *contents of* the dreaming. SGP proposes, as a philosophical interpretation, that waking physical reality stands to fundamental sentience the way dream-mountains stand to the dreamer: contained, not causing.

The analogy breaks where all analogies break: dreams are private, unstable, and lawless in ways physics is not. SGP's entire research program is the attempt to close that gap — to explain stability, shareability, and mathematical lawfulness from the starting point. The analogy motivates the question; it answers nothing.

## What this changes, and what it leaves unexplained

It changes the *direction of explanation*: instead of asking how objects generate experience, you ask how experience generates (stable, shared) objects. It reframes persistence — "what survives repeated interaction" (glossary:129–130) — as the central notion, rather than substance.

It leaves unexplained: *why* any content is stable at all; *why* different sentient perspectives encounter the *same* content (the sharing problem); and *why* the stable content is mathematical. Each "why" became a research thread. Stability became the closure program (Chapters 9–10). Sharing became the composition investigations (Chapter 12). Mathematicality became the physical-bridge program (Chapter 13). All three threads end in the same place — which is why this book exists.

## A word the book will not use

The records never call ordinary objects "illusions," and neither will this book. Experience-content can be fully real *as content* — the dream-mountain is really in the dream. The claim is about what is fundamental, not about what is fake. Readers who hear "unreal" should re-read the stipulation: containment is not dismissal.

## Where this chapter leaves you

We now have sentience, reality-as-sentience, and a world made of experience-content. The missing piece is mathematics: why should any of this be mathematically describable? Chapter 5 introduces possibility — and the hardest question of all.
