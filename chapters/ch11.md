# Chapter 11. What a Mathematical Representation Preserves

**The question:** When you describe two structures separately, then combine them, do you still know what you have? Sometimes the combination step silently discards the one piece of information that mattered.

## The T0 surprise

The fiber product decomposition (Chapters 9–10) broke on 10 cases at |S|=3 — and *all 10* shared one property: non-T0. (T0, plainly: the consequence-structure distinguishes every pair of dots — no two dots sit in exactly the same invariant sets. Posets always qualify; that is why Chapter 10 never saw the problem.) At |S|=4: zero violations among all 219 T0 systems [Finite-check]. T0 is not a technicality; it is the *exact boundary*: the paper's abstract and theorems were corrected to include it (R88/R89 records).

The audit traced the first failure to a single lemma — the Path Lemma (Lemma 3.3): it holds for all T0 systems tested and fails for all non-T0 ones. And the non-T0 fibers were completely classified: self-loop choices times strongly-connected digraphs — structured, but not Boolean (verified 10/10). No natural generalization exists. This is the program's cleanest boundary result: a hypothesis shown *necessary*, not merely sufficient, with the failure mechanism named.

## The composition boundary (C3)

A second representation result, equally sharp: if you classify two relations *separately* (their isomorphism classes) and then compose them, you cannot in general determine the composite's class — the *alignment* of the shared middle set is lost. Joint classification determines composition; separate classification does not (for |S| ≥ 2; the boundary sits exactly at |S| = 1). The lost information has a name: shared-carrier alignment.

Why does this matter to SGP? It was attacked as a possible source of novelty — perhaps "interaction" lives in the alignment that separate descriptions drop. The audit's answer: the boundary theorem is standard equivariance mathematics. Genuine, useful, and not SGP-specific [Negative on novelty; Math-verified on content]. It also did not move the Interaction priority score (see Chapter 12).

## The moral: representation is load-bearing

Two lessons the book carries forward. First, what survives a change of representation is the real content — and both results pass that test (they are representation-invariant theorems *about* representation). Second, the program kept hoping that the *gap* between a structure and its description would contain new mathematics. It contains real mathematics, but known mathematics. The gap is a workplace, not a goldmine.

## Where this chapter leaves you

Structure, order, boundaries: the program's mathematics is solid and standard. The promised land was always one rung higher — *interaction* and *selection*, where dynamics would favor some structures over others. Chapter 12 climbs there, and finds the ladder leaning against nothing.
