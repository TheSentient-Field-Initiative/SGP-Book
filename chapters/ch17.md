# Chapter 17. Why Every Electron Is Like Every Other Electron

**The delicate question:** The mission brief expected an Institute doctrine here — a "same particle" or "equation-idea" formulation, perhaps claiming electrons and protons are one underlying entity. An exhaustive search of the records found no such doctrine. This chapter says exactly what the records contain, explains the standard physics precisely, and refuses to invent the missing doctrine.

## The standard physics, stated exactly [Established]

- **All electrons are identical particles of the same species**: same mass, same charge, same spin. Ditto all protons. This is what "identical particles" means in quantum theory: swapping labels changes nothing observable, enforced by symmetrized states (bosons) or antisymmetrized states (fermions), organized in Fock space by occupation numbers.
- **Electrons and protons are NOT the same species.** They differ in mass (proton ≈ 1836× electron), electric charge (−1 vs +1), spin class (electron: elementary spin-1/2 fermion; proton: composite spin-1/2 hadron of quarks and gluons), and internal structure (electron: pointlike in the Standard Model; proton: bound quark-gluon system). (Figure 7 tabulates this.) Identical-particle theory never claims otherwise: it identifies particles *within* a species, never *across* species.

Two different claims must never be merged: (i) every electron is identical to every other electron — standard, true; (ii) electrons and protons are the same underlying entity — speculative, requiring new physics and new mathematics, found nowhere in the records.

## What the Institute actually wrote

The records' particle entry reads [SGP-interpretation]: "CANDIDATE 2: IDENTICAL PARTICLES — Object: Symmetrized N-particle state in Fock space. Represents: single state, no individual particle labels… ONE Fock state → N particles. PASS." (sgp_physics_cases_01.py:43, 226). That is claim (i), in standard formalism, with an SGP-style reading ("no individual labels" as another instance of one-whole→many-appearances). Pauli exclusion appears only as ordinary background (hydrogen shell structure in multi-electron treatments). No equation unifies electron and proton. No "same particle" doctrine exists: repo-wide searches for "same particle" return only null-model boilerplate; "equation-idea" and "every electron" return zero hits.

The closest match to "equation-idea" is the ONE EQUATION/ONE CONCEPT HYPOTHESIS (sgp_physics_cases_01.py:219–243): "Does ONE mathematical object describe ONE whole, while experience interprets it as multiple things?" — with all 17 candidates passing. Read it carefully: it is about *formalism* (one equation, many appearances), not *particle identity*. Conflating the two would manufacture exactly the doctrine the search failed to find. The book will not do that.

## Why the absence matters

A book hunting for SGP's boldest physics claim might be tempted to upgrade "no individual labels" into "all particles are one entity." The records decline the upgrade, and the downgrade audit agrees (ANALOGY, not evidence). The honest chapter is therefore short: standard indistinguishability, correctly stated, plus a documented absence where a legend might have grown. Negative search results are results.
