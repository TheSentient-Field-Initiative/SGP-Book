# Chapter 21. What Would Count as Progress?

**The final discipline:** a closed program must say what would reopen it — specifically, falsifiably, and without moving the goalposts later. RD-004 stated three conditions. This chapter explains them in plain language and refuses to add a fourth.

## Condition 1: a new primitive with independent motivation

Something mathematically real that ordinary relation theory lacks, needed for reasons *outside* SGP, and carrying physically specific information the null lacks (breaking I(T;S) = 0). Why this bar: the equivalence result is structural — only new information-content changes it. A relabeled relation does not qualify. Nothing in the records meets this.

## Condition 2: a falsifiable empirical prediction

A precise formalization yielding an observable the null does not share, testable with existing or near-future instruments. Why this bar: the Compass question is about the world, and only the world can overrule a null. Engineered predictions with added parameters do not qualify (Exp25 §13 showed why). Nothing in the records meets this.

## Condition 3: a non-circular combination solution

How micro-experience aggregates to macro-experience, without defining the conclusion into the premises, yielding new mathematics. Why this bar: observer→experience is the graph's deepest failure. Stipulated identities do not qualify. Nothing in the records meets this.

## What does NOT qualify (the anti-list)

No new analogy to a philosophical tradition. No new sharing/generality/imbalance bridge. No new viability simulation. No new reading of a stale priority score. And above all: not "more research might discover something." Hope is not a gate.

## The research the book does NOT propose

Deliberately, there is no "Experiment 26" in these pages. Proposing one to keep the program alive would violate the portfolio decision this book reports. The open-questions list (Appendix L) contains only questions meeting the book's own standard: clear object, noncircular method, decisive outcome — and it is short, because most candidates fail that standard.

## Where this chapter leaves you

The horizon is open but guarded: real conditions, real bars, no promises. The Epilogue returns to where you started — your own intuition that the world shouldn't be this intelligible — and tells you what the journey was for.
