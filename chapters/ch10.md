# Chapter 10. The Geometry of Consequences

**The puzzle:** Chapter 9 mapped fibers in general but left one question open: *how many* wirings share a given consequence-structure? For the special case of orderings — prerequisite chains, task hierarchies — the answer turns out to be exact.

## Partial orders and lower sets, plainly

A *partial order* is a ranking where some pairs compare and others don't: prerequisites ("learn fractions before algebra"), task dependencies, or simply 0 < 1 < 2 with gaps. A *lower set* is a collection closed downward: if a task is included, so are all its prerequisites. The lower sets of a poset form a closure system — our consequence-structure from Chapter 9, in ordered clothing.

## The manuscript's answer

The second manuscript (*Relations Inducing a Finite Poset's Lower-Set Closure*, papers/paper4_fiber_complexity/paper_v3.tex — verified against the source) proves [Math-verified-as-manuscript]:

> **Theorem.** For a finite partial order (S, ≤), the inducing relations form a fiber F in canonical bijection with P(S) × P(E): φ(A, E′) = {self-loops on A} ∪ T ∪ E′. Hence |F| = 2^|S|+|E|.

In words: every wiring with these consequences consists of three independent choices — which dots get self-loops (any subset A); the *covering* pairs T (mandatory: each prerequisite link with nothing between must be wired directly, since no detour exists); and any subset E′ of the *non-covering* comparable pairs E (optional shortcuts — a direct wire paralleling a chain changes nothing reachable).

The proof is four short paragraphs (verified in the source): loops never affect reachability; every inducing wire must run downhill (contained in R_≤); shortcut subsets preserve consequences via covering chains; covering pairs are unavoidable. Worked instances: a 3-chain gives |F| = 16; a 4-chain 128; a pure antichain 2^n; a V-shape (a<c, b<c) gives 8. The manuscript reports exhaustive checks over all finite partial orders on up to 5 elements (1, 3, 19, 219, 4231 — the standard labeled-poset counts) with zero violations [Finite-check]. (One provenance detail worth preserving: version 3 corrects version 2's abstract — "covering" to *non-covering* comparable pairs. The records correct themselves; the book keeps the correction visible.)

## New rung or same rung?

A genuine specialization that answers a question the general theory left open (the count). The abstract edge-classes of Chapter 9 — essential vs. conditionally removable — become concrete here: covering vs. non-covering pairs. The ladder grows by *specification*, not by new foundations. Same verdict as before, sharpened: beautiful order theory; no sentience in it.

## Where this chapter leaves you

We can now count wirings exactly — for ordered consequences. But a crack appeared in the general picture: the product decomposition fails for some consequence-structures. The failure has a name (non-T0), a precise location (one lemma), and a complete classification. Chapter 11 is about what representations preserve — and what they silently drop.
