# Chapter 3. Turning Reality Upside Down

**The reversal:** What if sentience doesn't come *from* the physical world — what if the physical world comes *from* sentience? This chapter explains SGP's founding stipulation and what it does and doesn't buy.

## The stipulation: reality is sentience

The second commitment in the Institute's five-part package [SGP-commitment]:

> "Reality is sentience (an identity claim: the fundamental nature of what exists is the capacity to respond)." (RESEARCH_DIRECTOR_STRATEGIC_REORIENTATION.md:346)

Earlier forms in the records include "REALITY = SENTIENCE" and, from a predecessor project, "Consciousness (sentience) is ground of being" (see research-map 03-terminology-map; Appendix B).

This is a *metaphysical starting commitment*, not a scientific result. Nobody observed reality being sentience; the program *starts* there, the way Euclid starts with axioms. The governance record frames the whole enterprise this way: sentience is "the starting point… not a hypothesis to be proved" (GOVERNANCE.md:19). You may accept or reject the starting point, but you cannot refute it with data — stipulations are not that kind of thing. (The book returns to this point in Chapter 19, because confusing "unfalsified" with "confirmed" is the central temptation of the whole story.)

## The ordinary picture, for contrast

*[Author-inference — illustration, not a record claim.]* The familiar physicalist picture runs the other way: matter is fundamental; complex arrangements of matter (brains) produce sentience as a late, rare product. On that view, feeling is something the universe *does*, in a few warm corners, after fourteen billion years. SGP reverses the arrow: sentience is what there *is*, and the physical world is something within it. Neither direction has been scientifically demonstrated; they are rival starting points. The book's job is not to pick your metaphysics but to track what each starting point lets you *derive* — because derivation, not stipulation, is where the program lives or dies.

## The category move

RD-001 names the delicate step: moving from "sentience is response" (a property *of systems*) to "reality is sentience" (the *nature of reality*) is a **category move** — from a property of things to what things fundamentally are (RD-001:355). This move was never examined as such until the strategic reviews. Under the phenomenal reading it yields something like Whitehead's metaphysics (feeling occasions as the stuff of the world); under the functional reading it yields "reality is relational structure," an existing structural-realist thesis relabeled. Either way, the move is an axiom, not a discovery. Axioms can be fruitful or barren. The rest of the book is the fruitfulness test.

## What the reversal changes — and what it doesn't

What it changes philosophically: the hard problem of consciousness (how does feeling arise from non-feeling matter?) dissolves, because matter was never fundamental. What replaces it is a mirror-image hard problem: how does stable, shared, mathematically describable *physicality* arise from sentience? The program traded one mystery for another — and then spent its research effort on the new one. That trade is the subject of Chapters 4–6.

What it doesn't change: any prediction, any measurement, any equation of physics. The reversal is interpretive. Two physicists can agree on every experiment while disagreeing about which direction the arrow points. This is why the book insists on evidence labels: the stipulation is [SGP-commitment], and nothing in the laboratory can promote it to [Established].

## Where this chapter leaves you

Reality-as-sentience is coherent as a starting point — and the strategic reviews confirm its coherence (RD-002). But coherence is cheap; the question is what the starting point *produces*. Chapter 4 asks what "the physical world" even means once reality is sentience.
