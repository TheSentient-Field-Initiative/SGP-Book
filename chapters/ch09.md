# Chapter 9. When Different Connections Produce the Same Result

**The intuitive idea:** Two road networks can serve the same cities. Two different wirings can light the same rooms. Two different relations can have the same *consequences* — and the family of relations sharing consequences can be mapped completely. That map is the Institute's strongest mathematical work.

## Closure in plain language

Fix a set S (dots) and a relation R (arrows). A subset A is *invariant* if no arrow leads out of it: R(A) ⊆ A — everyone you can reach from inside A is still inside A. The collection of all invariant subsets, Inv(R), is the relation's *consequence structure*: what stays put under the connections.

Two relations with the same invariant family are, for this purpose, interchangeable. The *fiber* over a consequence-structure C is the set F_C = {R : Inv(R) = C} — all wirings with the same consequences. The program's question: what does a fiber look like?

## The closure program: what was established

Working through dozens of numbered results (R19–R51; full index in Appendix D), the program established [Math-verified]:

- **Minimality (R29, corrected R30).** The invariant family is the *least* closure system consistent with the relation — the smallest consequence-structure containing everything the arrows force.
- **Collapse (R31).** Three descriptions — invariant subsets, closure system, closure operator — are one canonical object viewed three ways.
- **Universality (R40).** The construction works for *any* relation on *any* set, as a left adjoint (inclusion-reversing: the functor is contravariant, a correction the audits caught and kept).

And it falsified, cleanly [Negative]:

- **Reconstruction is FALSE (R35).** The hope: consequences determine wiring (up to renaming). The counterexample at |S|=3: f1 = {0↦1, 1↦0, 2↦1} and f2 = {0↦1, 1↦0, 2↦0} share I_f = {∅, {0,1}, S} but differ — the stray transient points at different partners. (26 such pairs at |S|=4.) Root cause: minimal invariant sets are *cycles*, not singletons. Consequences forget transients; dynamics remembers them. This is a real theorem-shaped hole: closure cannot see everything.
- **Convex sublattices FALSE (R46.1 → R50).** The conjecture that fibers are nicely convex sublattices died on 7 counterexamples at |S|=3 (intersection of relations can break the invariant family: Inv(R1∩R2) ⊋ Inv(R1)∩Inv(R2)).

## The repair: what fibers actually are

The maximum survived where the minimum didn't. A formula once claimed to give the *minimal* wiring (R44) actually gives the **unique MAXIMUM** R_C of the fiber (R45 correction; at |S|=3, 13 of 29 systems expose the error). And the fiber itself is a **finite poset with a unique maximum** — ordered by inclusion, topped by R_C, but not closed under union/intersection, not convex, not a lattice (R51 repair).
